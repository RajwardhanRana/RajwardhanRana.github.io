Week 1: Comparing living standards

Chart 1: Comparing GDP per capita

A bar chart comparing six countries, using the values included in the lecture example. The supplied data do not specify a year or units, so these values should be treated as a teaching example.

Chart 2: GDP per capita over time

A line chart comparing country trends. Its chart specification loads a CSV hosted by the Economics Observatory course repository and identifies the World Bank as the source. The original chart title and units are retained from the supplied example.

Chart 3: GDP and life expectancy

This scatter plot compares GDP per capita with life expectancy. Colour identifies continents and circle size represents population. Hover over a country to see its values. An association between income and life expectancy does not by itself establish causation.

Sources: World Bank and UN Population Prospects 2022, as identified in the course chart. View chart specification.

Week 2: Designing charts with India data

Two views of consumer-price inflation: comparing recent years and following the longer trend.

Chart 4: Comparing recent years

Horizontal bars compare India's annual inflation in 2020-2025. The zero baseline keeps bar lengths proportional to the values.

Chart 5: The longer trend

This chart follows India's annual consumer-price inflation from 2000 to 2025. A lower positive rate means prices are rising more slowly; it does not mean prices are falling.

Source: World Bank WDI, based on IMF International Financial Statistics. Annual percentage changes, not a price-index level. Snapshot downloaded 15 October 2026. This chart does not refresh automatically. Download cleaned CSV.

Seminar 2: chart adaptations and data preparation

The bar chart adapts the official simple bar example: changed title, teal colour, larger axis labels and swapped axes. Its example values are replaced with India inflation data.

The time series adapts the official line example. Changes include title and source subtitle, colour, line thickness, point markers, axis titles, label sizes, year formatting, lighter gridlines, tooltips and responsive width.

The source response was filtered to India observations from 2000 onward, sorted by year and exported as a comma-delimited CSV with Date, Year and Inflation columns. Dates use yyyy-mm-dd; January 1 represents each annual observation for plotting. Values remain in percentage units. Data preparation was performed with Python; the Excel cleaning exercise remains available to practise separately.

Chart specifications: bar chart JSON and line chart JSON.
